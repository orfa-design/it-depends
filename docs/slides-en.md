## Slide 2 — Problem

**Why doesn't intention become action?**

- A designer already uses AI every day. Not a beginner.
- Sees a colleague ship something real with AI — and freezes.
- The fear hits *before* opening any tool. "This isn't for me."

> Not laziness. A UX problem of the first step.

*(screenshot: optional)*

---

## Slide 3 — Hypothesis

**Lower the cost of entry**

- The problem isn't a lack of information — the first step feels too big.
- We lower the activation energy, before the brain talks itself out of it.
- Not "learn AI" — stop feeling left out.

> JTBD: "When I see colleagues making something real with AI → I want to take the
> first step from words to a living result → so I stop feeling this isn't for me."

## Slide 4 — Research

**The solution is earned, not guessed**

- Base: 1 in-depth interview · survey of 10 · competitor analysis.
- 4 hypotheses → filtered with a "how do I know?" test → 2 critical ones tested in interview.
- H2 casual→power ✅ · H5 capability blindness ✅ · H4 refined (latent ideas).

## Slide 5 — Team

**Process is engineering too**

Two people, two separate Claude sessions, one repo as a shared brain.

- **Shared brain** — GitHub: branches, merges, auto-deploy to Vercel.
- **Robot teammate** — hooks: block editing on a stale version, remind us to log.
- **Thinking ≠ decisions** — raw ideas → synthesis → agreed becomes a decision.

> Rules are intentions. Automation is a guarantee.
